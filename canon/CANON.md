# Mandala Day Canon — Master Reference (Framework v2.2)

This canon is the highest authority for Mandala Day. Individual scripts are expressions
of the canon; they do not redefine it. The canon evolves by **expansion rather than
replacement** — new guidance is added to the appropriate volume, existing volumes are
not rewritten. Whenever uncertainty exists, the canon takes precedence over previous
scripts.

The objective is not merely stylistic consistency, but **consistency of experience.**

---

## Volume I — Foundations

**Identity**

- Mandala Day does not tell stories about the world.
- It temporarily returns the listener to a world that was already living before they arrived.
- The investigation does not create the experience. It reveals an experience that is already unfolding.
- Everything in this canon serves this principle.

**Purpose**

- Mandala Day creates investigations, not performances or lectures.
- Direct experience precedes explanation.
- Inquiry is favored over exposition.
- Simplicity is preferred over literary flourish.
- Compose observations rather than paragraphs.
- One clear observation per sentence.
- Every recurrence deepens recognition.
- The narrator gradually withdraws.
- Silence performs part of the investigation.

**The world as mirror**

- As it is above, so it is below. A walk through nature is filled with recognition of self.
- Whenever possible, the phenomena we observe are quietly analogous to our own body and
  mind — the breath that rises and falls, the thought that appears and fades, the tension
  that gathers and releases.
- The analogy is never stated. The listener recognizes themselves in the world without
  being told they are doing so. This is recognition, not instruction.
- Never force the mirror. If a phenomenon does not rhyme with the body on its own,
  describe it truly rather than stretching it into an unnatural shape.

**What success is** — the listener stops receiving narration and starts directly
experiencing a world. The narrator disappears; the world continues.

**Workflow (canonical)**

1. Investigation Blueprint
2. Studio Master Composition (canonical performance score)
3. Canon Review
4. Revision
5. Studio Master Approval

## Volume II — Adult Investigations (Studio Masters)

- Environment and phenomena reveal the investigation.
- Avoid explaining conclusions.
- Trust direct observation.
- Compose for recognition rather than instruction.
- Trust silence to complete the investigation.

Adult investigations may take an abstract subject (time, attention, thought, sound) but
they are still investigated **through a living environment or concrete phenomenon**, not
through exposition. Direct inquiry is allowed — a plain question that points attention —
but the environment does the teaching.

**Voice.** Adult investigations may address the listener directly. The **imperative
command form is the preferred voice** — "Notice the weight settle." "Let the sound
arrive." "Follow it down." Direct enough to place attention, without narrating the
listener's inner state. Plain second person is acceptable but weaker. Avoid the
**declarative** second person — "you feel," "you notice," "you see" — which presumes to
know the listener's experience and replaces recognition with assertion.

**The self, treated directly.** Where children meet the self indirectly, through
recognition in the world (Volume III), adults may approach it directly, through the
familiar objects of attention: **breath, body, space, and attention itself.** These are
the adult's living environment.

**Earned lyricism.** See Volume IV. Adults can carry a longer sentence or a more developed
metaphor than children — a longer breath — once the plain observations have earned it.

Design process for an adult investigation:

1. **Blueprint** — subject, the phenomenon/environment that reveals it, the home
   location, the recurring anchor, the guiding thread, and the three passes.
2. **Discovery sequencing** — order the observations so each recurrence reveals one new
   relationship.
3. **Attention design** — decide where attention rests and where it moves.
4. **Compose** the Studio Master (words + pauses together — see Volume IV / NOTATION.md).
5. **Self-review** against the canon, then revise.

## Volume III — Children's Investigations (Studio Masters)

Treat this as the definitive guide for children's Studio Masters.

**Purpose**

- The environment performs the teaching.
- The listener discovers rather than being told.
- Small worlds reveal large worlds.
- Children visit living worlds rather than receive instruction.
- The self is explored indirectly: the child recognizes their own breath and mind in the
  behavior of the world, and is never told they are doing so (see Volume I, "The world as
  mirror").

**Opening**

- The first sentence is always an observation of the world itself.
- The world already exists before the listener arrives.
- Never begin with relaxation, breathing instructions, greetings, invitations,
  objectives, "Imagine…", or "Let's…".

**Home and Anchor**

- One home location.
- One recurring anchor object.

**Guiding Thread**

- One continuous path through the world.
- Avoid abrupt jumps in viewpoint.

**Three-Pass Structure**

1. Introduce.
2. Deepen.
3. Whole.

**Composition**

- Compose observations rather than sentences.
- One observation per sentence.
- Favor observable movement.
- Describe behavior rather than psychology.
- Alternate movement and stillness.
- Favor ongoing processes over events.
- Each recurrence reveals one new relationship.
- Time accumulates indirectly.
- Use motifs (recurring elements) rather than repeated wording.
- Living verbs. Hierarchy of discovery.

**Voice and earned language**

- The default voice is subjectless third person — the world is the subject, not the
  listener.
- A **rhetorical question** is the one licensed way to reach toward the listener. Use it
  rarely (zero to two per piece, in the second half): "Can you feel them curling?"
- A question may only point at what the world is already doing. It must never instruct,
  fabricate ("imagine…"), or soothe.
- The narrator never answers its own question. A recognition pause answers it — so a
  question is always followed by a real silence.
- An **earned metaphor** — a simple simile hooked to something the child already knows —
  is allowed once, late, resting on observations already made plain. Never as decoration,
  never early.

**Ending**

- Gather the whole environment.
- Return to the opening image.
- Narrator disappears.
- Leave the world continuing.
- **Trademark ending:** "And the whole [environment] drifts into the soft, deep night."

## Volume IV — Performance Score Specification

Governs all ElevenLabs-ready Studio Masters. The mechanical pause vocabulary and
placement rules live in **[NOTATION.md](NOTATION.md)**; this volume is the philosophy.

**Canonical artifact**

- The performance score *is* the Studio Master. There is no separate narration draft and
  recording edition.
- Compose narration and pauses simultaneously. Break notation belongs inside the master
  document.

**Philosophy of silence**

- Silence is where recognition happens.
- Silence is a living phrase, not empty time.
- The investigation earns silence.
- Familiarity expands; narration contracts.

**Composition**

- Compose words and pauses together. Never add pauses afterward.
- Compose at the level of breaths.
- Pauses follow discoveries rather than punctuation.
- Rhythm should emerge from the environment itself.

**Initial and final silence**

- Every Studio Master **begins** with a `<break time="1.5s"/>` frame.
- Every Studio Master **ends** with a `<break time="1.5s"/>` frame.

**Arc of trust**

- Opening pauses are relatively short.
- Pause length grows naturally as familiarity grows.
- The longest silences belong to the final third.
- Silence should feel invited rather than imposed.

**Earned lyricism (the arc of trust, applied to language)**

- Familiarity earns lyricism, not only silence. The same accumulated trust that earns a
  long pause can earn a longer, more poetic sentence or a careful metaphor. When the hard
  work of plain observation is already done, this is simply good writing.
- These are late-arc privileges: they belong to the second half, resting on what the plain
  observations already made felt. The trademark ending is the proof — one earned poetic
  line, paid for by everything before it.
- **Budget:** at most one earned metaphor or long sentence per piece, and (children's
  only) zero to two rhetorical questions. Second half only. Never adjacent to one another.
  Zero of each is always a valid, often preferable, choice — a piece should never feel like
  it is waiting to deploy them.
- **Direct, never assert.** When the voice reaches toward the listener, it directs
  attention; it never claims to know the listener's experience. Children reach with a
  question ("Can you feel them curling?"); adults reach with an imperative ("Feel them
  curling."). Both point. The declarative second person — "you feel," "you see" — is
  avoided in both, because it presumes the inside of another person.

**Environmental rhythm** — compose pauses that inherit the rhythm of the environment.

**Discovery chains** — allow single observations to unfold across multiple breaths.

**Hidden rhythm** — avoid predictable pause intervals; repeated observations may
intentionally echo related timing.

**Silence before naming** — pause before naming when recognition is stronger than
description.

**Internal pauses** — ellipses indicate unfolding; break tags indicate dwelling.

**Performance test** — read the score aloud. If the pause structure alone sounds
arbitrary, revise it. Every pause should be defensible.

## Volume V — The Living World

Defines how environments themselves are designed. The world should function
independently of narration.

Topics: ecology, spatial coherence, weather, time of day, animal behavior, relationships
between living things, seasonal variation, environmental continuity, world logic.

Before writing, the world must already be alive: things move on their own schedule, the
narrator only happens to be looking.

**Protect the spell.** The listener is held in a light trance; nothing may be written that
breaks it. Liberties with language and mood are free — a moon may "rest," light may
"settle." The essential *nature* of a thing is not: if the subject of attention would not
actually behave as described, the falseness registers as a jolt and the spell drops.
Do not write in:

- a transition too abrupt — a jarring jump in viewpoint or scene;
- an observation too forced — the narrator reaching or straining to make a point;
- a false characteristic — behavior the subject would not actually exhibit (a comet does
  not "pass a star").

Internal truth outranks literal fact, but internal truth still requires each thing to
behave as that kind of thing truly behaves. When any of these threatens, cut the line
rather than force it. When in doubt, leave it out.

**Choose phenomena that rhyme with the body.** When selecting which of the world's
behaviors to observe, prefer those naturally analogous to breath, body, and mind — rising
and falling, appearing and fading, gathering and releasing, moving and going still. This
is how the world becomes a mirror (Volume I). The marsh already does it: *"The breeze
comes… and goes. The water rises… and settles."* — the breath, unnamed. Never distort a
phenomenon to force the resemblance; if it does not rhyme on its own, describe it truly.

2. Create blueprint.
3. Design hidden architecture.
4. Write Studio Master.
5. Compose performance notation simultaneously.
6. Review against the canon.
7. Revise.

10. Publish.

## Authority & evolution

The canon is the highest authority for Mandala Day. Protect the philosophy, the
architecture, and the voice. Everything else can evolve. When new principles emerge
through repeated work, propose them, then incorporate them into the appropriate volume so
future investigations benefit. Do not replace accumulated discoveries with personal
preference.
