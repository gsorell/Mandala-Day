# The Quiet Hall — Studio Master (draft)

- **Audience:** children (bedtime)
- **Title:** *The Quiet Hall*
- **Status:** **rendered and wired into the app.** Runs **7:05.5** — the first kids' master
  to land on the 7:00 target without a trim.
- **Calibration result:** 44 spoken lines / 196s of notated break time → 425.5s, i.e.
  **~5.2s per line**, against the 5.4s predicted from Rain on the Roof (estimate was
  ≈7:14, actual 7:05.5 — under by 9s). The lean composition is what did it: budget
  **~43–45 lines against ~195s of break for a true 7:00**, and prefer whole observations
  to short fragment-lines, which is where Rain lost its time.
- **Slug:** `the-quiet-hall` · **templateId:** `extra_the_quiet_hall` · **abbr:** `QH`

## Blueprint

- **Subject:** a room built for sitting, at the hour when the day's sitting is over — a
  place that goes on being still after everyone has gone.
- **Environment:** a long wooden meditation hall at dusk, door open to a garden. The word
  *zendo* is never used and no instruction is ever given: the room simply *is* what it is.
  This is the first shipped kids' piece set in a made room rather than a landscape, and the
  first whose central event is a **sound** rather than a motion.
- **Home:** the hall itself, seen from inside — a **resting gaze** (like Where the Stars
  Turn / Rain on the Roof). The listener stays; the evening comes to them.
- **Anchor:** **the small candle on the low table** — the flame that leans when the air
  moves and stands tall again. Returned to in every pass and in the close; it is the last
  thing still burning at the end.
- **Guiding thread:** the evening arriving — one last stripe of low light crossing the
  floor, climbing the wall, thinning, and going, until only candlelight is left.
- **Six recurring elements:** the candle flame (anchor — leans · stands · steadies) · the
  stripe of low light on the wooden floor (the clock of the piece) · **the dust turning in
  the light** (drifts out and is gone, drifts in and shines — the hide-and-seek) · the round
  cushions in two rows (the still ones; each with a hollow worn in over many days) · **the
  far bell in the garden** (sounds once, fills the room, thins past hearing) · the small
  brown moth on the doorframe (wings open… and close — the breath, unnamed).
- **The mirror, kept implicit (Vol I):** the flame leaning and standing is the breath; the
  bell arriving and thinning away is a thought appearing and passing; the dust showing only
  where the light reaches is attention itself; the empty cushions with their worn hollows
  are rest that has been practiced a long time. None named.
- **The return-and-settle beat:** the bell. It is the only event in the piece and it comes
  from *outside* the room — the world reaching in through the open door — and what it does
  is disappear so gradually that its ending cannot be located. The question rides it.
- **Earned language, on budget (Vol IV):** one rhetorical question — "Can you still hear
  it?" (final third; rides the real fading the bell just did; unanswered → 6s silence) —
  and one metaphor — the candlelight on the far wall, "soft and round, like a small moon in
  the room" (late, simple, hooked to something a child knows). Five lines apart, never
  adjacent.
- **Three passes:** *Introduce* (the room, the open door, the candle, the light on the
  floor, the dust, the cushions, the moth) · *Deepen* (the light reaches the wall and
  climbs; the dust vanishes where the light has left and shows again near the candle; a
  cool draft bends the flame low and it steadies; the cushions wait in the growing dark) ·
  *Whole* (the bell → the question → the moth's wings close for good → the last light slips
  off the wall → only the candle → the metaphor → gather in echoed 2.5s beats → 12s
  immersion → return to the opening image → trademark close with the caesura).

## If the render comes in short

Deepen, never pad. The natural room to add: a second return to the bell's silence before
the gather (the garden going quiet around where the sound was), and one more beat on the
cushions in the dark. Do not add a second question or a second metaphor — the budget is
spent.
