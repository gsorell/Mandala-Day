## Blueprint

- **Subject:** a place built for noise and brightness, being gentle when no one is watching.
  The park does not *become* peaceful at closing time — it is revealed to have been a quiet
  place all along, underneath the noise.
- **Environment:** a small amusement park in the last hour of a summer night, after the
  gate has shut. **Deliberately generic** — a carousel, a great wheel, lanterns over a lane,
  booths, boats on dark water. No named park, character, ride, or trademark anywhere in the
  score, and nothing that reads as one specific real place.
- **Home:** the middle of the park, at the carousel. A **resting gaze** whose attention
  glides outward — carousel → lane → booths → wheel → water — and comes home in the gather
  (the Where the Stars Turn structure, not the marsh's traveling thread).
- **Anchor:** **one small painted horse on the carousel** — it rises and falls while the
  ride turns, rises a little less as it slows, and comes to rest low and stays there. It is
  the breath, and the piece's most direct body-rhyme; it returns in every pass and opens the
  gather.
- **Guiding thread:** the park winding down — motion draining out of it a piece at a time,
  and the lights going down section by section until only the lanterns are left.
- **Six recurring elements:** the small painted horse (anchor — rises · falls · comes to
  rest) · the string of lanterns over the lane (sways, then only shines) · the paper flags
  on the booth roofs (lift… and lie down — the breath again) · the great wheel (its lights
  sliding around the rim, then holding still) · the boats on dark water (knock softly, stop,
  the water goes smooth) · **the ducks and the sparrows** — the animals that come out
  *because* the people have gone.
- **The eerie problem, and the fix:** an empty fairground can read as abandoned rather than
  restful. Three things keep it warm: the lanterns stay lit, so nothing is ever left fully
  dark; the animals arrive and take the place over; and the last motion in the piece is a
  breeze, not a silence. The emptiness is the park being *let alone*, not deserted.
- **The one event:** the big lights going out reveals the stars over the carousel — they
  were always there, hidden by the park's own glare. This is Volume I made literal: the
  investigation does not create the experience, it reveals one already unfolding. It is also
  deliberately a different device from The Quiet Hall's fading bell — that piece's event was
  a sound disappearing, this one's is a thing appearing. (The carousel's music stops in a
  single line and is never dwelt on, precisely so it does not re-run the bell.)
- **The mirror, kept implicit (Vol I):** the horse rising and falling is the breath; the
  wheel's long turn slowing to a stop is the body settling; the water smoothing until it can
  hold a reflection is the mind going still; the animals returning when the noise ends is
  ease coming back when effort stops. None named.
- **Earned language, on budget (Vol IV):** one rhetorical question — "Were they there all
  along?" (final third; points only at what the world just did; unanswered → 6s silence; and
  it is the first question in the set that is not "can you hear/feel/find it," so the device
  does not wear) — and one metaphor — the still carousel "like a music box with its lid
  closed" (late, simple, and honest: a carousel really is a music box). Three lines apart.
- **Three passes:** *Introduce* (the park at closing, the carousel and the horse, the
  lanterns, the flags, the wheel, the boats) · *Deepen* (the carousel slows and the horse
  settles; the music stops; the horses stand in a ring; the lanterns hang still; the wheel's
  last turn; the boats stop and the water smooths; the ducks climb the empty dock; the
  sparrows go quiet) · *Whole* (the lights go out → the stars come out → the question → the
  water holds both → the metaphor → gather in echoed 2.5s beats → 12s immersion → return to
  the opening image → trademark close with the caesura).

> **The close is the reference performance for the closing descent**
> ([../NOTATION.md](../NOTATION.md)). The trademark line is split into **three** units across
> **two** untagged blank lines, with a single ellipsis inside the last — and the comma after
> *soft* is kept, so the phrase stays grammatically open across the breath and the descent
> resumes rather than restarts. Do not replace either blank line with a break tag, and do not
> flatten the last gap into a blank line. The silence before the line is also held at `12s`
> rather than the usual `10s`, so the close arrives out of a deeper quiet. **This piece is
> where the descent rule came from** — arrived at by ear on the render, after an
> all-ellipses version still read brisk.

## If the render comes in short

Deepen, never pad. Room to add, in order: a beat on the great wheel's empty seats rocking
and going still; one more return to the horse before the lights go out; a longer hold after
the stars appear. Do not add a second question or a second metaphor — the budget is spent.
